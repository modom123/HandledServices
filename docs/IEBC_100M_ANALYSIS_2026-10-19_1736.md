# IEBC: what it takes to reach $100M in 5 years

## 1. What IEBC is today (from the files)

IEBC is a Detroit company (48219) founded in 2025 by Mark Odom. Right now it is mainly one person. It has four business lines that don't yet fit together:

| Line | What exists | How it earns | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. IEBC Enterprise Suite** (accounting, payroll, HR, invoicing, tax, 4 AI advisors) | A working prototype: one 108 KB `server.js`, one 168 KB `index.html`, SQLite, demo data | SaaS at $49 / $99 / $199 per org per month | Prototype, no paying customers on record |
| **B. IEBC AI Remote Workforce** (named AI employees such as Eleanor Wei, CFO, and Arthur Vance, dispatch) | Built into Handled through `/api/iebc/v1`, with scopes, an approval queue and a usage meter | $2,000–$5,000 per agent per month; 10% of net take from Handled | In production at one internal customer (Handled) |
| **C. Handled** (an Uber/Angi-style home and business services marketplace in Metro Detroit) | A full app: booking, pricing, dispatch, QA, payouts, B2B, gov-contract search | 15–35% take on each job | Pre-launch or very early |
| **D. Consulting and government contracts** (marketing and communications RFPs, SAM.gov) | Proposal drafts for City of Detroit RFPs | Project fees | The RFPs need 5+ years of experience, and one has already closed |

**Biggest finding:** the asset worth the most is **B, the AI workforce, proven on C, Handled**. A is a commodity product in a crowded market, and D is a small cash-flow business. A $100M plan has to pick one engine.

## 2. First, define "$100M"

This choice changes the plan more than anything else:

| Target | What it means | Realistic in 5 years? |
|---|---|---|
| **$100M annual revenue** | About 1 in 1,000 venture-backed startups reaches this in 5 years. It takes $50–150M+ raised and a team of 300–600. | Very unlikely from a solo start. Possible only with a breakout product and large funding. |
| **$100M company valuation** | At typical multiples: AI/SaaS about 8–12x ARR needs **~$8–12M ARR**; a marketplace about 3–5x net revenue needs ~$20–30M take. | **Hard, but achievable** with focus and one funding round |
| **$100M in gross bookings (GMV)** through Handled | What customers pay, not what IEBC keeps. At a 20% take, that is $20M of revenue. | Possible with B2B accounts and 5–8 cities. Handled's own plan reaches $50M GMV in year 5. |

**Recommendation:** aim for a **$100M valuation by 2031**, which means about **$10M ARR from the AI workforce plus $50–100M GMV through Handled**. Treat $100M of revenue as a 10-year goal. Handled's own plan already reaches $100M take only in year 10.

## 4. Challenges

### Business and strategy
1. **Too many lines for a one-person company.** Accounting SaaS, AI agents, a services marketplace and gov consulting would each need a full team. Spreading effort this thin is the main risk.
2. **"Competing with NetSuite" doesn't match the product.** NetSuite sells for $1,000–$10,000+ a month to mid-market companies with ERP, inventory, multi-entity books and audit-grade controls. A $49–$199 SQLite app competes with QuickBooks Online and Wave, not NetSuite. Investors and buyers will notice the mismatch.
3. **No track record yet.** IEBC began in 2025. RFP 550575 requires 5 years of experience. The proposal notes say the "50+ AI agents" and "22 hours/week saved per client" claims need proof, and the phone number differs between documents (661.466.9932 vs. 206.850.5327). These have to be fixed before outreach at scale.
4. **AI agents are getting cheaper to build.** Microsoft, Salesforce Agentforce, Intuit Assist and many startups now sell AI agents. "Named AI employee" products such as Artisan and 11x exist, and some have been criticized for overstated results. IEBC's edge has to be **measured outcomes in a vertical** (services businesses), not the agents themselves.
5. **Capital.** Handled's own plan says the $100M path "needs outside capital." The bootstrap case reaches $4.1M of take in year 5.

### Product and technology (from the zip's code)
6. **Payroll is a placeholder, and real payroll carries legal risk.** `server.js:423` uses a flat 16% federal rate, 7.65% FICA and a flat state rate. There are no W-4s, brackets, wage bases, local taxes, filings or deposits. If customers ran real payroll on this, IEBC would face tax penalties and liability. Partner instead: Gusto Embedded, Check or Mineral.
7. **Bank sync is simulated.** There is a `plaid_id` column, but the "sync" isn't real. Accounting that doesn't connect to banks won't keep customers.
8. **The architecture won't scale.** It is one server process with one SQLite file and no horizontal scaling, background queue, backups or migrations. That is fine for a demo but won't support thousands of tenants or SOC 2. Handled already runs on Supabase/Postgres plus Vercel, so the Suite should move to that stack.
9. **Security gaps to close before selling.**
   - The JWT secret falls back to a value hard-coded in the code (`server.js:29`).
   - The seeded demo login is `admin@demo.com` / `demo1234`.
   - One `MASTER_HUB_KEY` is shared by every client deployment.
   - There is no MFA, no SSO and no SOC 2. Accounting and payroll buyers ask for SOC 2 Type II.
10. **The AI model ID is out of date.** The code pins `claude-sonnet-4-20250514`, and older model IDs are eventually retired. Move the model ID into config and use a current model.

### Market and operations
11. **Handled's risks.** Worker classification (1099 pros), keeping pros supplied, insurance and claims, local customer-acquisition costs, and Angi, Thumbtack, Taskrabbit and Amazon Home Services in the same market.
12. **Owner time.** Every human approval in the AI-workforce design, every RFP and every B2B close currently depends on one person.

## 5. Opportunities

1. **AI Back Office for service businesses.** This is the $100M-valuation engine. Sell exactly what runs Handled to cleaning, landscaping, HVAC, junk-removal and property-maintenance companies: intake, quoting, dispatch, QA, collections and pro compliance. That market has millions of small US operators, and ServiceTitan, Jobber and Housecall Pro have proven they will pay for software.
   - IEBC can sell *done-for-you labor*, not just software.
   - The offer: "Replace a $45k/yr office coordinator for $1,500–$3,500/mo, with an approval queue and an audit log."
   - Handled's live AI-driven rate, profit per job and revenue per employee are the case study.
2. **Handled as proof and second engine.** A strong Detroit market is the demo every buyer of B wants to see. B2B accounts (property managers, unit turnovers, storage cleanouts, HOAs) carry the volume, and gov janitorial and grounds contracts on SAM.gov (561720, 561730) add to it.
3. **Turn the Suite (A) into the agents' system of record.** Don't sell it as "NetSuite for $49." Bundle the books, invoicing and receipt AI into the Workforce subscription and integrate with QuickBooks and Xero instead of replacing them. That cuts churn and avoids a fight with Intuit.
4. **Government and certifications as a moat.** Detroit-Headquartered and Detroit Resident Business certifications are worth up to 10 bid points. Add SBA 8(a) or other set-asides if IEBC qualifies, plus MBE/DBE (Michigan) and SAM.gov registration. Win first contracts by teaming as a sub or joint venture with established firms (+2 JV points), and build past performance.
5. **Channel partners.** Accountants and bookkeepers, franchise systems (one franchisor deal can add 50–500 locations), trade associations (ISSA for cleaning, NALP for landscaping), and the Detroit economic-development groups (TechTown, Detroit Means Business, MEDC).
6. **Roll up service companies.** Later, buy small cleaning or landscaping companies at 2–4x EBITDA and run them on the IEBC workforce to raise margins. Lenders and private equity understand this model, and it adds revenue faster than organic growth alone.

## 7. Next 90 days

1. **Pick the engine:** the AI Back Office for service businesses. Pause marketing the Suite as a "NetSuite competitor" and don't sell payroll until a payroll partner is in place.
2. **Fix credibility:** one phone number and one bio; replace unproven claims with "designed to" or real numbers from Handled.
3. **Close the security issues** in item 9 and the payroll issue in item 6 before any outside customer logs in.
4. **Sign 3 design-partner clients** (local cleaning or landscaping companies) at a discount in return for case-study rights.
5. **Launch Handled's Detroit soft launch** and report its metrics weekly. Those numbers are IEBC's pitch.
6. **Register:** SAM.gov entity and Detroit CRIO certifications; find one established firm to team with on the next City RFP.
7. **Hire or partner for the first role**: a sales and partnerships lead, so the owner isn't the only bottleneck.

---

**Bottom line:**
- $100M of *revenue* in 5 years is very unlikely for a one-person 2025 start.
- A **$100M company** by 2031 is a realistic, hard target. To get there, IEBC sells its AI workforce to service businesses (~$10M ARR), uses Handled as the live proof (and a second revenue line), and stops spreading effort across four lines.
