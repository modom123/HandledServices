# Handled — Business Plan

**Home & business services. Handled.** One company and one app for cleaning, windows, carpets, gutters,
lawn, trees, dog-waste removal, junk and large-item removal, handyman work, and kitchen, bath and
whole-home remodels. Every job is done by vetted subcontractors and run by AI. A small human team
manages it, and IEBC's AI workforce staffs the back office.

---

## 1. The model

| | Angi (lead marketplace) | ServiceTitan (contractor software) | **Handled** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who it serves | Homeowner gets 3–5 contractor calls | The contractor's back office | **The customer, end to end** |
| How it earns | Sells leads to pros | SaaS fee per tech | **15–35% of every completed job, taken off the top (like Uber)** |
| Price | Quotes after calls | n/a | **Instant upfront price (pricing engine + AI photo review)** |
| Accountability | None after the lead | n/a | **We dispatch, QA with photos, charge after QA, 30-day guarantee** |
| Pros | Pay for leads | Pay for software | **Pay nothing; get pre-sold jobs and fast payouts** |

**It works like Uber.** We find the customer, price the job, connect them to a vetted subcontractor and
take our cut off the top. The customer pays us; we pay the pro what's left.

| Service | Pro gets | **We keep** |
|---|---|---|
| House & office cleaning, windows, carpets, gutters, junk & large-item removal | 65% | **35%** |
| Lawn care, dog-poop removal, handyman | 70% | **30%** |
| Tree removal & trimming | 75% | **25%** |
| Bath, kitchen & whole-home remodels | 85% | **15%** |

### Why no job can lose money (enforced in code and in the database)
1. **Our take is always 15–35% of the price.** Every price, whether instant, AI-adjusted, set after a site visit or edited by staff, is split by one function (`splitJob` in `packages/core/src/pricing.ts`). The database also rejects any job or payout that would leave us below 15% (`supabase/migrations/20261001183000_take_rate_guard.sql`).
2. **Payouts round down**, so rounding can never cost us money.
3. **Pros are paid only from money we've collected.** A card that charges successfully releases the payout. If the charge fails, the payout is held. Invoiced jobs stay pending until payment arrives.
4. **No lead costs, no truck, no inventory.** Pros bring their own tools, insurance and vehicles, and do the free site visits for remodels and tree work.
5. **Guarantee redos are done by the original pro at no extra payout**; that's in the pro agreement. A 1% reserve covers the rare refund.
6. **Card fees (about 3%) come out of our take**, and every service still earns money after fees (unit-tested). Remodels and large invoices use ACH bank payments (about $5 per payment) instead of cards.

**Why it wins:** the work that makes service businesses expensive to run is intake, quoting,
scheduling, dispatch, follow-up, QA, collections and payouts. That is exactly the work AI now does well.
A lead site can't do it because it never owns the job. Contractor software can't do it because it never
owns the customer. We own both.

### Revenue streams
1. **Recurring home plans**: cleaning, lawn and dog-waste removal on weekly, biweekly or monthly plans (10–20% plan discount). This is the base of the business, with predictable revenue and high lifetime value.
2. **One-time jobs**: junk and large-item removal, gutters, windows, carpets, handyman, tree work.
3. **Remodels**: kitchen, bath and whole-home. High tickets at our 15% floor, sold through a free site visit and paid by milestone over ACH.
4. **Commercial accounts**: offices, retail, restaurants, property managers and HOAs. One vendor for every site, net terms and an SLA dashboard.
5. *(Later)* Membership ("Handled+": priority booking and an annual home check-up), and resale of IEBC's operating stack to other service brands.

## 2. 80% AI-driven — the operating design

**Definition (measured live in the Handled Hub):** the *AI-driven rate* is the share of completed jobs
that went from booking to paid with **zero human touches**. **Target: ≥ 80%.** A human touch is any manual
edit, dispatch, QA approval or status change by staff (`apps/web/lib/metrics.ts`). Actions by IEBC's AI
employees don't count as human touches. Approvals of their high-risk actions do.

| Function | Who does it | Human role |
|---|---|---|
| Customer intake, Q&A, instant price | AI concierge (web + app) + pricing engine | — |
| Quote review from notes & photos | AI quote (Claude), clamped to ±40% guardrails | Firm price after site visits (remodel, tree) |
| Scheduling & dispatch | AI dispatcher (ranking + offers + auto re-dispatch on expiry) | Exceptions only (no eligible pro) |
| Customer updates, reminders, messages | Automated timeline + email; IEBC **Diego Martinez** (client care) | Escalations |
| Quality assurance | AI photo QA on every job | Review the ~10–20% the AI flags |
| Billing & collections | Stripe card-on-file, charged automatically after QA | Failed-charge follow-up |
| Pro payouts | Approved automatically when QA passes | Weekly payout run |
| Pro recruiting & screening | Website application + AI screen; IEBC **Tyler Walsh** | Final approval, interviews |
| Pro compliance | IEBC **Marcus Hill** flags expiring insurance | Verify COI and background check |
| Daily ops management | AI morning brief + 15-min sweep + IEBC **Arthur Vance** (dispatch) | Read the brief, clear alerts |
| Finance & pricing | IEBC **Eleanor Wei** (CFO agent), Finance page | Approve price changes and payouts |
| B2B sales | IEBC **Elena Markov** (accounts), **Clara Dubois** (lead gen) | Close and sign contracts |

## 3. IEBC Remote Workforce — the showcase

Handled is IEBC's flagship proof that a **remote AI workforce can run a real company**:

- Nine IEBC AI employees are assigned to Handled departments (Handled Hub → **IEBC Workforce**). Each has scoped permissions and an autonomy level: *suggest*, *act with approval* or *autonomous*.
- They act through the IEBC Workforce API (`/api/iebc/v1`). Every call is logged, and high-risk actions (price changes, payouts, cancellations, activating pros, marketing emails) always wait for a human.
- A **usage meter** (calls and executed actions per agent per month) supports invoicing.

**Billing recommendation:** for Handled, IEBC bills a **revenue share: 10% of Handled's net take**
(after card fees and reserve) instead of fixed seats. It grows with the business, and like everything
else here it can never put Handled in the red. For outside companies, IEBC sells the same workforce
per seat at **$2,000–$5,000 per agent per month**. Handled's results (AI-driven rate, profit per job,
revenue per employee) are the case study. The usage meter in the Handled Hub shows the work behind
each invoice.

## 4. Five-year plan — profitable every year

### How to read the numbers
- **Bookings (GMV):** everything customers pay. This is the "size" of the business.
- **Our take:** our 15–35% cut. This is our actual revenue, the way Uber reports it.
- **Net take:** our take minus card fees and the 1% guarantee reserve.
- **The spending rule:** total operating costs stay at or below **85% of net take**, so **at least 15% of net take is profit every year**. People, marketing, the IEBC workforce, tech and overhead each get a fixed share. We hire and spend only as the take grows.

**Volume assumptions:** recurring-plan customer about $1,900/yr (mix of biweekly cleaning, weekly lawn and
dog-poop plans). One-time job about $290. Remodel about $24,000. Commercial account about $1,800/mo.

**Every job earns money, and every year is in the black.** The trade-off is speed: because we only spend
what the business earns, year 1 is lean. The founder is paid from profit, and marketing has about $35 to win
each new customer. That means leaning on free and cheap channels in year 1: a Google Business Profile, Nextdoor,
referral credits, property managers, and IEBC's outreach agents (Clara Dubois, Elena Markov). If customers
cost more than planned to win, growth slows down; the business doesn't go into the red.

**The only money needed up front** is about $15–25k of one-time startup costs before the first job: the LLC,
the first insurance premium, a legal review of the pro agreement, the domain and branding. That's an
investment, not an operating loss.

### What "$10M" means — pick the target
| Target | When this plan gets there | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| **$10M in bookings** (what customers pay through us) | **Early year 5** ($9M in year 4, $13.5M in year 5) | The plan above |
| **$10M in our take** (our actual revenue) | About year 8 at this pace | About $33M in bookings: roughly 18 metros, or a much larger commercial book |

### Biggest levers to beat the plan
1. **Recurring mix.** Every one-time customer gets an AI-timed offer to switch to a plan. Plans are the cheapest revenue to keep.
2. **Commercial accounts.** One property manager can equal 50 homes, at no extra marketing cost.
3. **Keep the take near the top of the band.** Cleaning and hauling at 35%, remodels never below 15%.
4. **Route density.** Pros earn more per hour when their jobs are close together, which keeps good pros loyal without lowering our cut.
5. **Metro playbook.** A new metro opens only when existing metros' profit can fund it: about 25 vetted pros, local ads and one launcher.

### Risks & mitigations
- **Worker classification:** pros are independent businesses with their own insurance and tools. They set their availability and accept or decline jobs. Get a labor-law review per state before launch.
- **Licensing:** remodel, tree work and some handyman tasks need licensed or insured contractors. The platform blocks dispatch to pros with expired insurance.
- **Quality incidents:** photo QA, a 30-day guarantee, a pro rating floor and automatic suspension rules.
- **AI errors:** price guardrails (±40% of the rules engine), human approval for every high-risk action, and full audit logs (`ai_runs`, `agent_actions`).
