# Bid engine: how to bid correctly

Every bid goes through the same steps in **Hub → 📝 Bids**. A bid **can't be marked submitted** until every step is done; the server enforces this, not just the screen. The banner at the top of each bid lists exactly what's still missing.

## Start a bid
- **City, county, state, school or other:** Hub → Bids → *Start a bid*. Enter the title, agency, RFQ number, due date and link.
- **Federal:** open the notice in Hub → Gov contracts and press *Start a bid*. The details carry over.
- Each new bid comes with the standard compliance items for its source:
  - **City:** schedule / prequalified list approval, supplier portal registration, tax clearances, City affidavits.
  - **Federal:** SAM.gov registration, size standard, limits on subcontracting, wage determination.

## 1. Go / no-go
Answer the five must-pass questions: eligible, staffed, insured, profitable, time.
- Any **no** marks the bid *No bid*. Write down why; it helps next time.
- All **yes** moves the bid to pricing.
- **Eligibility comes first.** An ineligible bid is thrown out unread. For example, Detroit's Supply Schedule #1 RFQs are open only to approved vendors.

## 2. Documents
- Upload the solicitation, **every addendum**, and the price form as PDFs. Save Word and Excel files as PDF first. Files go straight to private storage, up to 50 MB each.
- Press **Read the solicitation into the matrix**. The AI lists every requirement, form, date, insurance and bond item, and scoring factor. It also copies the price form's lines into pricing and fills in the due date and submit method.
- **When a new addendum comes out:** upload it and run the AI again. Only new items are added.

## 3. Compliance matrix
- Check an item off only after **you've seen it in the documents** and know where our response answers it. Write that location in "Answered in", for example "Section 4" or "Form B".
- Add anything the AI missed by hand.
- Changing an item clears the review sign-off.

## 4. Pricing
How each price line is built:
1. Start with the **pro's cost per unit**, plus materials.
2. Add supervision, insurance, admin and contingency (and bonds, if any).
3. Add the **cost of waiting to be paid.** Government pays about 30–60 days after a correct invoice; we pay pros right away.
4. Add our **margin as a percentage of the price**, then round up.

Warnings appear when:
- A line has no pro price.
- A line's margin is under the **8% floor**. Going below it needs an owner override.
- A price is **more than 15% above the last award**, or our cost is above it.

The bottom row shows the yearly price, the contract total, profit, and the **cash we'll carry** before the agency pays.

Copy **Bid / unit** onto the agency's own price form, in its units.

## 5. Pros' written prices
- **Find pros to ask** lists approved pros whose trades match the price lines.
- **Email price requests** sends each pro a private link where they enter:
  - their own price per line
  - capacity
  - small-business status
  - confirmation that their prices hold for 120 days and the first contract year
- It's a quote, not an offer of work. If we win, the work is offered as a written subcontract the pro can accept or decline.
- **Use the lowest committed prices** fills in the pro cost for each line.
- A line with only one committed pro shows **no backup**. Get a second pro before you bid.

## 6. Review & submit
1. **Review.** Six checks, ideally by someone who didn't write the bid: the matrix against the documents, addenda checked today, the price form re-added by hand, forms signed, the format matches the instructions, and the pros have the terms in writing. Any later change to pricing or the matrix clears the sign-off.
2. **Submit** exactly as the instructions say, **at least one business day early**.
3. **Upload the confirmation** (receipt, email or portal screenshot) under Documents → Submission confirmation.
4. **Mark submitted.**

## 7. Result & debrief
- Record won or lost, the winner, the winning total, and the **winning unit prices** from the bid tabulation. These become benchmarks for the next bid.
- Ask the agency for a debrief either way, and write down what to change next time.
