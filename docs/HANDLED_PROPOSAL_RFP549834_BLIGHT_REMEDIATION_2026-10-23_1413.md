## B4. Quality Sequence (built on the City's Quality of Work matrix)

Every crew is trained on, and every supervisor checks, this sequence:

1. **Line of sight first.** Look at the property from the street as a resident would. Everything visible gets remediated.
2. **Litter and debris removed BEFORE any cutting or weed whipping.** No shredded litter left in the grass.
3. **Alley center to curb line.** The full responsibility zone, including sidewalk cracks, curb lines, fence lines and building foundations ("Front Stage").
4. **Around every obstacle.** Curbs, poles, bollards, trees and shrubs are trimmed by hand.
5. **Complete removal of overgrowth,** including vegetation and trees grown into fences.
6. **Posts and poles removed 4–6" below grade,** holes backfilled, and concrete or asphalt patched to match.
7. **Wrap-up.** Clippings blown off sidewalks and streets, no paint overspray or drips, site broom-clean.
8. **Openings boarded to "harden the target"** per USFA Board-Up Procedures: painted pressure-treated CDX plywood (or approved material) cut to fit, secured with carriage bolts and 2×4 strongbacks from inside or tamper-resistant screws, painted to match.

**Quality control.** Supervisors self-score every job on the City's matrix (Yes/No for each line) before the completion package is sent. Each week we review at least [10]% of completed jobs on site. Any "No" is corrected at no cost to the City within 2 business days. Results go into the weekly report, so the City's end-of-contract quality score has data behind it.

## B8. Biohazard and sharps plan

At no additional cost to the City:

1. **Training:** every crew member completes bloodborne-pathogen training (MIOSHA Part 554) and receives the hepatitis B vaccine offer.
2. **Detection:** crews scan the site visually before any debris removal or mowing at encampment sites, alleys and vacant lots, and cut nothing until the area is cleared of sharps.
3. **PPE:** puncture-resistant gloves, safety glasses, boots, Tyvek suits where needed. Tongs or grabbers are used; sharps are never handled by hand.
4. **Containment:** sharps go into rigid, puncture-resistant, labeled sharps containers. Other biohazardous waste (human waste, blood-soaked materials) is double-bagged in red biohazard bags, kept separate from all other debris.
5. **Disposal:** through a licensed medical-waste hauler, **[NAME]**, under Michigan's Medical Waste Regulatory Act, with manifests kept on file.
6. **Exposure response:** a written exposure control plan; any needle-stick is reported to the supervisor at once and the worker gets same-day medical evaluation.
7. **Encampments:** cleared only when directed by the City, coordinated with City outreach and the Police Department. Personal property is handled per the City's direction.

## B9. Safety and compliance

- **MIOSHA / OSHA:** written safety program, job hazard assessments, PPE, required MIOSHA posters, training in a language each worker understands, and a General Duty Clause commitment to a hazard-free workplace.
- **Electrical:** no sign, light or fixture is removed until our licensed electrician has identified, isolated and de-energized it (lockout/tagout). DTE is contacted for utility-side disconnects. **MISS DIG 811** is called before any excavation, post removal or auger work.
- **Lead-safe (EPA RRP):** pre-1978 structures are presumed to have lead paint unless tested. Painting and power washing follow RRP containment, wet methods, HEPA cleanup and cleaning verification, under an EPA-certified firm and renovator.
- **Herbicides:** applied only by MDARD-certified applicators, following the label and Michigan's Natural Resources and Environmental Protection Act (Part 83), with application records kept.
- **Traffic:** work in the right-of-way follows the Michigan Manual of Uniform Traffic Control Devices; lane closures are coordinated with the City.
- **Paint:** the City's approved palette (Attachment H, 2025) only; colors confirmed with the City before work starts.
- **Board-up:** USFA Board-Up Procedures (Appendix A).

## C2. How these prices were built (internal: delete before submission)

- **Crew cost:** 4-person crew at a loaded ~$31/hour per worker (wage + payroll tax + workers' comp + PPE/training), so ~$124 per crew-hour in labor. Truck, trailer, fuel and small equipment add ~$45 per crew-hour. Overhead (insurance incl. pollution, supervision, documentation) adds 18%. Target margin 15–20%.
- **Disposal:** metro Detroit landfill / transfer tipping about $45–$65 per ton. Mixed debris runs about 0.3–0.5 ton per cubic yard, so roughly $15–$30 per cubic yard in fees before labor and hauling.
- **Hourly items (16, 17)** are priced **per worker-hour** pending the City's answer to Question 2. If the City means per crew-hour, multiply by 4 (≈ $248 / $356).
- **Items 15, 37, 38** are mostly subcontracted. Replace with real subcontractor quotes plus 10–15% markup.
- **Item 29** threshold is ambiguous (Question 3); the price assumes one standard opening up to one 4' × 8' sheet.
- Prices are a starting point for what Detroit blight contracts typically pay. Compare them with the City's prior award prices (request past bid tabulations through Bonfire or FOIA) and the JV partner's own history before submitting.
